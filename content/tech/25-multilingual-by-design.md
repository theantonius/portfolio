Working with two collaborators from Integrow Math Solutions, I built technical prototypes for LessonLift through NYC FIRST. We explored how computer vision and teacher-reviewed observations could support K–2 math instruction for multilingual learners. Our project was selected as a finalist in the Multilingual by Design grant competition.

The demos include live finger tracking with MediaPipe running in the browser, recorded computer vision examples, and interactive teacher workflows. The latest interface pairs a recorded lesson with editable observations: teachers can replay a moment, accept or revise an observation, and record a per-student formative assessment.

The teacher-review demo uses prepared transcript entries and sample observations; summary updates are simulated in JavaScript. The live hand-tracking demo performs real on-device inference. These are separate demonstrations, rather than a complete system that automatically analyzes new classroom recordings and generates assessments or lesson plans.

The diagrams describe our proposed assessment and privacy workflows. They show the intended direction, including teacher review and local processing; the complete de-identification, storage and retention pipeline was not validated in this prototype.
